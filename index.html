PlayInsight: turning match footage into player analytics

I lead engineering on PlayInsight, a video analytics platform for badminton. A coach or player uploads a match filmed on one phone, or on up to four phones around the court. PlayInsight finds the players, the shuttle and the rackets, reconstructs the court in 3D, splits the match into rallies, classifies every stroke, and reports distance, speed and shot mix for each player.

What I built

  • Custom models. Shuttle detection and 5-keypoint racket pose models trained on annotated match footage, alongside YOLOv8-pose and RTMPose for players.
  • Court geometry. Calibration solves the full 3D camera pose from court lines and net posts, then derives the floor homography and net plane from it. Outlier points are rejected and per-point reprojection error is shown to the user.
  • Multi-camera tracking. Two to four phones around the court are synced automatically by matching the sound of racket hits, then every player and shuttle position is triangulated from all cameras that see it. When a player is hidden behind their partner or the net post from one angle, another camera supplies the position, and the shuttle gets a true 3D height that one camera can't measure.
  • Player identity. Players are assigned to fixed court-side slots. When two partners overlap and separate, jersey colour histograms decide who is who, so stats stay attached to the right person.
  • Stroke classification. Each hit is detected from a wrist-velocity peak and classified as smash, drop, clear, lift, net shot or drive from wrist height relative to shoulders and hips and the swing direction. Players also get per-frame stances: ready, lunge, jump, reach, crouch.
  • Rally detection. A state machine with four start rules and five end rules (shuttle above the net, feet still, bounce, timeouts). Every decision is stored with its reason, so a wrong call can be replayed and explained.
  • Platform. Multi-GB uploads, match sessions that group several camera angles, a GPU job queue with live progress, cancel and retry, highlight video export with overlays rendered in, player profiles that track stats across matches, Stripe subscriptions with plan limits, and role-based permissions.
  • Viewer. Ten overlay layers drawn on canvas (skeleton, shuttle trail, heatmap, minimap and more), follow cam, frame stepping, and a 3D replay built from MotionBERT pose lifting.

Decisions

Decision

Serverless GPU workers behind a queue

Analysis runs on autoscaling GPU workers fed by a Redis queue. The load is bursty: a match needs minutes of GPU time, then nothing. Scaling to zero keeps cost proportional to use, and the queue keeps the API fast and makes jobs cancellable and retryable.

Decision

Fuse cameras after analysis, not inside the tracker

Each camera runs the normal single-camera pipeline; a separate fusion step combines the results. Trackers and the rally state machine are stateful per video stream. Making them multi-view aware would have meant rewriting them. Fusing afterwards kept them untouched, and the output keeps the same format, so the viewer needed no changes.

Decision

Sync cameras by the sound of the shuttle

Offsets come from cross-correlating band-passed energy envelopes of each camera's audio. Raw waveform correlation failed on real footage (confidence 0.01–0.03) because rallies are full of near-identical hit sounds. Filtering to impact frequencies gave confidence of 0.18–0.84 with one dominant peak, and anything below the threshold falls back to a manual scrubber.

Decision

Ship results as a SQLite file instead of an API

Each analysis is one .analysis.db in object storage. The viewer reads thousands of rows per second while someone scrubs the video. Through REST that is slow and expensive; a file cached at the CDN and queried in the browser is fast and costs nothing per request.

Decision

Choose the 3D model by measurement

We compared MotionBERT's mesh and pose models against the real 2D input. The mesh model correlated 0.26 with the input's X axis, the pose model 0.9988. We shipped the pose model and documented the remaining torso-tilt bias as a single-camera limit, with multi-camera triangulation as the fix.

Vocatech: taking over a telecom platform and making it reliable

Vocatech sells business phone service built on Cisco BroadWorks. I inherited six codebases from earlier developers, some only ever edited on the server, and became the engineer responsible for all of them.

The systems

  • TextDock (344 commits): the Webex embedded app, automatic per-customer spaces, WhatsApp merged into the same routing, and a full 10DLC compliance workflow with The Campaign Registry and Bandwidth.
  • Admin portal (276 commits in a 6-developer repo): BroadWorks PBX management. I extracted one reusable scheduling engine and rolled it out to more than 12 call features. I also added bulk extension creation, contact sync over API, CSV and SFTP, and call reports with stored OpenAI transcripts.
  • Public REST API (built from scratch): Slim 4 on Cloud Run with OpenAPI docs, token auth, AES-encrypted API keys, call and message reports, and outbound webhooks dispatched by Cloud Scheduler.
  • Website and internal tools: marketing site performance and SEO, visitor attribution, and a LinkedIn engagement tool that drafts comments with Gemini.

Decisions

Decision

Sync Webex by webhook instead of polling

Membership and message events update local tables; the UI reads from MySQL. The app had called Webex once per room on every load, so it slowed down and hit rate limits as customers added spaces.

Decision

Replace session auth with tokens in the embedded app

Short-lived tokens with rotation that is safe under parallel calls. Sessions expired mid-use inside the Webex embedded app and broke when users switched accounts in the same app.

Decision

One scheduling engine instead of a dozen copies

Time and holiday schedules with recurrence became one service used by every call feature. Each feature had its own copy of the schedule UI and save logic, so every schedule bug had to be fixed page by page.

Decision

Batch webhook delivery with concurrent HTTP pools

The dispatcher sends to subscribers in parallel through Guzzle pools, triggered by Cloud Scheduler and tracked in delivery tables. With sequential delivery, every new subscriber made each run longer.

Taking over safely

The first commit in several of these repos is "latest code from server". Before changing behaviour I added Docker for local development, setup docs, a changelog with semantic versions, git hooks for cache busting, and access rules that keep logs, SQL dumps and credential files out of the web root.

Promoted You Sync: stopping a product feed from silently losing data

Product data lives in a Google Sheet, prices come from the StickerYou API, and the merged catalogue goes to three distributor platforms: ASI, OneSource and Distributor Central. The original code was hard to change, and distributors sometimes lost fields nobody had touched.

What I changed

  • Found the data loss. ASI's update endpoint replaces the whole product, so any field the sync didn't send was erased. I changed every write to read the current record first and merge (GET before POST), and added a check that the merge never drops real ASI data.
  • Rebuilt around channels. Each destination is a self-contained plugin with its own validation, token handling and export rules, on a shared domain model with unit tests. Adding a distributor no longer touches the others.
  • Made failures traceable. Every export or pricing error points back to the product that caused it. OneSource categories are checked against its approved list before sending. Products priced in several sizes became one SKU with several price grids.
  • Upgraded the main site from PHP 7.2 to 8.3 with a written regression checklist covering quotes, PDFs, auth and emails.

Debugging log

symptom, cause, fix
PlayInsight
2026

Two cameras, but only 9% of shuttle points triangulated

Symptom
Each camera tracked the shuttle well on its own, yet the fused result had almost no 3D shuttle positions.
Cause
A small, fast shuttle is detected on only a fraction of sampled frames per camera. Triangulation needed both cameras to hit the exact same frame, and two independent sparse streams rarely line up.
Fix
Bridge short gaps in each camera's track by interpolating between real detections, triangulate from those, and flag interpolated points so the viewer never presents them as raw detections.
PlayInsight
Aug 2026

One rally for an entire match

Symptom
Some matches came back as a single rally lasting the whole video.
Cause
The floor homography and the net position were fitted separately from the same clicks. When they disagreed, the net line projected off-screen, so "shuttle above net" was never true and the rally never ended.
Fix
Solve the 3D camera pose once and derive both from it. If the pose can't be solved, fall back to the default court rather than a half-fitted one.
PlayInsight
Aug 2026

A setting that was never saved

Symptom
None yet. Found while making frame sampling configurable.
Cause
The engine never wrote frame_skip into the results metadata; it always stored the default of 3. The viewer syncs overlays using that value, so any other setting would have drifted silently.
Fix
Pass the value through and store it. Fixed before it could ship.

How I work

Write the cause down

Deploys, monitoring and the follow-up after launch are part of the job. I've run my own infrastructure on every recent project.
